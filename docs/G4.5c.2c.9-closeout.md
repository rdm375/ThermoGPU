## Findings

1. Specializing the calculation while preserving the 176-byte output record substantially reduces executed instructions and kernel time.
2. Padded and compact kernels have the same register count, stack allocation and measured local-memory sector counts.
3. Padded and full kernels generate identical global-store sector counts, while padded and compact have nearly identical kernel execution times.
4. Compact output substantially reduces global-store activity and device-to-host transfer volume.
5. DRAM-write ratios must not be interpreted as ratios of logical output bytes because caching and memory-system behavior affect the counters.

## Conclusion

The controlled experiment strongly supports computational specialization as the dominant source of the observed kernel-speed improvement under the tested conditions. The results do not uniquely separate the effects of reduced arithmetic and reduced register pressure.

Compact output remains important for host transfers. Property-specific EOS evaluation is the preferred direction for future GPU-resident solver integration.
